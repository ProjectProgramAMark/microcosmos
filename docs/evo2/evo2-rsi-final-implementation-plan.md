# Evo²-Ecosystem: Final RSI Implementation Plan

Date: 2026-07-13  
Status: implementation-ready prospective follow-up  
Project: **Evo²-Ecosystem: Recursive Discovery of Heredity Policies for Embodied Artificial Life**

## 1. Executive decision

Continue the existing Evo²-Ecosystem project. Do not replace its simulator,
controller, heredity operators, evaluator architecture, or completed pilot.

The completed pilot established a functioning bounded program-improvement loop:

- embodied CPPN-controlled filament organisms live, consume finite resources,
  reproduce, mutate, and die in Microcosmos;
- direct fixed-shape TensorNEAT CPPNs are inherited between organisms;
- ShinkaEvolve edits an ecology-conditioned heredity scheduler;
- trusted code applies clone, parametric, structural, or mixed TensorNEAT
  mutation;
- stable-trained and catastrophe-trained program searches were evaluated under
  frozen, integrity-checked protocols.

The pilot's sealed result was scientifically negative: cloning and conventional
parametric mutation remained competitive, and the catastrophe-trained Shinka
program did not reliably outperform its stable-trained counterpart or the
strong baselines. Preserve that result unchanged. Treat it as the causal
motivation for this prospective follow-up, not as an experiment to be repaired
retroactively.

The follow-up strengthens the project in two ways:

1. **Repair the biological verifier.** Begin every world from a clonal CPPN
   population and impose a persistent actuator regime shift that applies to all
   current and future organisms. This removes standing genetic variation as an
   explanation for recovery and makes newly generated heredity causally useful.
2. **Test the improvement engine directly.** Evaluate the actual parent chain
   of Shinka-generated heredity programs on completely fresh populations,
   unseen founders, unseen ecological seeds, and an unseen injury location.

The central confirmatory question is:

> **Can bounded program evolution improve an ecology-conditioned heredity
> algorithm itself, as measured by whether a descendant program enables fresh
> clonal CPPN populations to acquire heritable recovery from unseen persistent
> actuator damage better than its initial program ancestor under fixed
> simulation and mutation-action budgets?**

The project must not pursue broader rewriting merely to make the system appear
more recursive. The strongest RSI evidence comes from a causal verifier,
fresh-population transfer, an authenticated program lineage, and fixed budgets.

## 2. Problem statement

### 2.1 What the pilot proved

The pilot proved that the engineering loop works:

```text
ShinkaEvolve changes heredity code
                 ↓
trusted code selects and applies TensorNEAT mutation
                 ↓
fresh embodied populations live and reproduce
                 ↓
ecological adaptation is measured executably
                 ↓
candidate results guide later program proposals
```

It also established the relevant integrity boundary. Candidate code cannot
rewrite Microcosmos physics, TensorNEAT mutation kernels, resources, lifecycle
rules, catastrophe transforms, metrics, manifests, budgets, or hidden seeds.

### 2.2 Why the pilot did not prove a better improvement mechanism

The original worlds began with a deliberately varied panel of viable founder
CPPNs. Clone-only heredity therefore inherited useful standing diversity. A
population could recover through ecological sorting among pre-existing founder
lineages without the heredity scheduler creating any useful novelty.

That made the benchmark a test of population robustness, but not a sufficiently
sharp test of mutation-policy quality. The null result is consequently
informative: it revealed that the verifier did not causally require the artifact
that Shinka was meant to improve.

### 2.3 Why the proposed follow-up fixes the causal weakness

Every ecosystem in the new study begins from genetically identical organisms.
A persistent actuator degradation then changes the controller-to-body mapping
for every organism, including children born after the shock.

Clone-only heredity cannot create genetic variation. If a mutating heredity
policy recovers better than clone, and post-injury descendants outperform their
exact pre-injury ancestors in a common garden, the result supports actual
heritable adaptation rather than standing variation, physiological reset at
birth, or demographic turnover.

## 3. Outcome hypothesis

If the ecosystem begins from clonal founders and experiences persistent actuator
degradation, then useful recovery should require newly generated heritable CPPN
variation. If ShinkaEvolve is evaluated on this adaptation-demanding benchmark,
then descendants in its program archive may become better heredity algorithms
than their program ancestors because the executable verifier now rewards the
capacity to produce future adaptive descendants.

The hypothesis is falsified for this study if the final selected program does
not show a reproducible sealed advantage over its initial program ancestor. It
is not sufficient for the final program to produce one impressive population,
to beat only one seed, or to have a higher training score.

## 4. Evidence hierarchy and claims

The claims are deliberately tiered. Do not redefine success after results are
known.

| Tier | Claim | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A | The benchmark requires useful heredity | Injury hurts clone; clone remains genetically invariant; at least one conventional mutation policy beats clone with a positive paired interval; births and post-injury generations are sufficient |
| B | The outer program lineage improved | Final punctuated-trained program beats its exact initial program ancestor on fresh sealed shocked-world AUC under matched budgets |
| C | The improvement is meaningful relative to conventional evolution | Final punctuated-trained program beats the strongest predeclared compute-matched conventional heredity baseline |
| D | Punctuated training helped this selected search case | Final punctuated-trained program beats the final stable-trained program on the shared sealed injury panel |
| E | The discovered code mechanism matters | One predeclared or fresh-panel one-factor ablation removes or reduces the program's paired advantage |
| F | The biological change is inherited and injury-specific | Exact post-injury organism descendants beat their matched pre-injury ancestors in the injured common garden, preferably with a positive injured-versus-healthy interaction |

Tier A is a prerequisite for running Shinka. Tier B is the confirmatory bounded
program-improvement claim. Tiers C through F strengthen the scientific result
but are not to be forced by modifying the evaluator.

With only one matched stable/punctuated pair of outer searches, Tier D is a
selected-search case study. A general claim about the superiority of punctuated
outer training would require multiple independent outer-search seeds.

## 10. Persistent actuator-regime implementation

### 10.1 State representation

Add one world-level field to `EcosystemState`:

```python
actuator_gain: jax.Array  # [hinges_per_organism], float32
```

This vector represents the efficacy of each local bending hinge in the current
world. It is deliberately not indexed by organism slot. The same physical
regime applies to every current organism and every child born later.

At reset:

```python
actuator_gain = jnp.ones((hinges_per_organism,), dtype=jnp.float32)
```

At the event boundary, trusted event code replaces it with the frozen injury
vector. No birth code needs to copy or reset it.

### 10.2 Action application

The current CPPN controller produces one intended bending delta for every
flattened hinge. Broadcast the world vector without changing shapes:

```python
gain = jnp.broadcast_to(
    state.actuator_gain[None, :],
    (max_creatures, hinges_per_organism),
).reshape(-1)

intended_bend = controller_bend_action + optional_external_bend
effective_bend = intended_bend * gain
```

Apply `effective_bend` to the bending rest angles.

Charge actuation energy from the **intended** command, using the same frozen cost
formula as the pilot. Do not compute metabolic cost from `effective_bend`.
Otherwise actuator damage would reduce energy expenditure and could create an
unintended ecological advantage for injured organisms.

If the public environment action path is retained, injury must scale the total
actuator intent rather than only the CPPN component; no alternative action path
may bypass the regime shift.

### 10.3 Event representation

Add one strict event kind:

```python
EventKind.ACTUATOR_INJURY = "actuator_injury"
```

Use explicit frozen local hinge gains in the manifest rather than deriving a
region from ambiguous percentages:

```json
{
  "event_kind": "actuator_injury",
  "event_parameters": {
    "hinge_gains": [0.2, 0.2, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]
  }
}
```

Validation requirements:

- the vector length exactly equals `hinges_per_organism` for the declared body
  layout;
- every value is finite and lies in `[0, 1]`;
- at least one value is `< 1` for an injured event;
- a sham/null event leaves the all-ones vector unchanged;
- vector values and order are part of canonical manifest bytes and hashes.

### 10.4 Spatial transfer partitions

Freeze contiguous injuries over the small number of physical hinges:

- training: head-side contiguous hinges;
- development: middle contiguous hinges;
- sealed: tail-side contiguous hinges.

Use exact hinge indices, not runtime percentages. If more than one pattern per
partition is affordable, vary contiguous width while retaining the same injury
mechanism. Do not introduce arbitrary masks or a learned environment generator.

The injury is persistent for the remainder of the episode. Candidate code never
receives the pattern identity or event time.

### 10.5 Stable-arm sham event

The stable arm must use the same event boundary, horizon, score code, chunking,
founders, and seeds as the punctuated arm. At the boundary it applies a sham
event that leaves gains at `1.0`.

The only intended training-treatment difference is therefore:

```text
stable:     all actuator gains remain 1.0
punctuated: frozen training injury vector becomes active
```

Use a small frozen set of event times rather than one universally repeated tick
if the existing manifest capacity permits it. The candidate does not receive
absolute time, so it must react to ecological consequences instead of memorizing
a schedule.

### 10.6 Differentiability and accelerator claim

Multiplication by a fixed actuator-gain vector is JAX-native, GPU-native, and
differentiable with respect to controller output between discrete transitions.
Changing the gain at an event boundary remains a discrete state transition.

Continue the accurate project claim:

> CPPN inference, actuator scaling, physics, resources, lifecycle state, and
> trusted mutation execution use fixed-shape JAX arrays and run on GPU. The
> complete ecology contains discrete birth, death, mutation, graph, and
> catastrophe transitions and is not claimed to be end-to-end differentiable.

## 11. Clonal founder implementation

### 11.1 Preserve existing diverse-founder behavior

The existing `initialize_cppn_population` deliberately varies living founders.
Keep that mode so the pilot and ordinary ecosystem demos retain their semantics.

Extend the initializer or environment constructor with one explicit optional
founder input rather than creating a parallel initializer:

```python
def initialize_cppn_population(
    capacity: int,
    initial_population: int,
    *,
    founder_genome: CPPNGenome | None = None,
) -> CPPNGenome:
    ...
```

Behavior:

- `founder_genome is None`: retain the existing varied-founder panel exactly;
- a founder is supplied: validate/canonicalize once and broadcast the identical
  genome into every initially living slot;
- inactive slots retain legal fixed-shape values but remain physically inactive.

The reset path receives an already loaded frozen founder and does not generate a
new founder from the ecological world seed.

### 11.2 Founder-genome and founder-lineage semantics

Clonal genotype and phylogenetic identity are different concepts:

- every initially living organism receives the same frozen founder genome;
- retain the existing unique `founder_lineage_id` for each initial individual;
- descendants inherit the parent branch's lineage ID;
- the manifest-level `founder_id` and `founder_sha256` identify the shared
  genotype used by the whole world.

This preserves existing lineage-entropy and dominant-branch telemetry while
removing all initial genetic variation. Do not interpret lineage entropy as
genetic diversity in the clonal experiment. Detailed finalist ancestry continues
to use individual and parent IDs.

Create one immutable founder bank under the existing Evo² experiment tree, for

### 11.4 Founder generation and screening

Generate a deterministic candidate founder pool using trusted TensorNEAT
operators and fixed seeds. Freeze the generator source and seeds.

Screen founders only for:

- graph/cache validity;
- finite rollout state;
- minimum uninjured productivity;
- sufficient uninjured survival and reproduction under a frozen reference
  policy;
- nonidentity between founder hashes.

Never select development or sealed founders based on actuator-injury recovery.
In particular, sealed founders may be checked for uninjured viability but not for
which heredity policy adapts best to the sealed injury.

### 11.5 Manifest identity

Every world declares both ecological and genetic identity:

```json

  "founder_sha256": "...",
  "world_seed": 101,
  "pair_id": "train-00-seed-101"
}
```

The founder hash is verified before environment construction. Training,
development, and sealed founder IDs and hashes must be disjoint.

## 12. Benchmark causality gate

Do not run Shinka until this gate passes on the prospective benchmark.

### 12.1 Policies

Evaluate at minimum:

1. clone;
2. fixed parametric mutation;
3. fixed structural mutation;
4. fixed mixed mutation.

Use the existing trusted operators without changing their constants.

### 12.2 Exact paired design

For each training founder and world seed:

1. run one common pre-event ecosystem trajectory;
2. fork the exact state at the event boundary;
3. apply either persistent actuator injury or the all-ones sham event;
4. run the identical fixed post-event horizon;
5. evaluate every policy on paired founder×seed worlds.

Policies use the same founder artifacts, scenario seeds, injury vectors, event
boundaries, and simulator budgets. Mutation streams remain policy-specific
because mutation is the treatment.

### 12.3 Predefined calibration grid

Keep the pilot ecology and lifecycle constants fixed. The primary calibration
dimension is injury gain:

```text
0.1, 0.2, 0.4
```

Use one predeclared default event-time distribution. Only if it produces too few
post-event births under every mutation policy may one predeclared later event
schedule be attempted. Do not begin retuning resources, metabolism, birth cost,
mutation constants, and score weights.

Select the **strongest** injury from the predefined grid that satisfies all gate
conditions. Record every attempted configuration, including failures, in an
append-only calibration manifest.

### 12.4 Gate conditions

The training benchmark passes only when all of the following hold:

1. Every clone birth is bitwise genome-identical to its parent and initial
   founder.
2. Injury produces a meaningful clone deficit relative to its paired sham world;
   the paired 95% interval for `injured - sham` excludes zero in the harmful
   direction.
3. At least one conventional mutation policy has a positive direct injured-world
   AUC advantage over clone, with a paired hierarchical-bootstrap 95% interval
   whose lower bound is greater than zero.
4. At least one mutation policy retains a predeclared viable survival floor.
5. The median injured world for that policy contains at least four post-event
   births and reaches at least two post-event generations beyond the generation
   present at the event boundary.
6. All runs remain finite and integrity-valid.
7. The selected injury does not change array shapes or introduce a new
   compilation per founder or policy.

The exact survival floor and post-event horizon must be written into the
calibration preregistration before running the grid. If existing pilot variance
supports a precision calculation, use it to set the number of founder×seed
pairs.

### 12.5 Development confirmation

After choosing one training configuration:

- freeze its event vectors, timing rule, horizon, score, and hashes;
- run it once on the development founders using the same clone and conventional
  mutation policies;
- require the qualitative heredity-sensitive relationship to transfer;
- do not retune after viewing development results unless a specific fallback was
  preregistered before development access.

If the benchmark fails development, report failed transfer and stop or invoke
the single preregistered fallback. Do not repeatedly tune until it passes.

## 13. Heredity-policy interface

### 13.1 Default: retain the existing four-score scheduler

The default scientific experiment uses the existing candidate ABI:

```python
def make_offspring(
    parent_genome_summary,
    parent_stats,
    population_stats,
    rng,
):
    """Return scores for clone, parametric, structural, and mixed mutation."""
    return jnp.asarray([clone, parametric, structural, mixed], dtype=jnp.float32)
```

Trusted code:

1. requires exactly four finite values;
2. clips them to the existing score bounds;
3. uses deterministic `argmax`;
4. applies one trusted TensorNEAT operator;
5. transforms and validates the resulting graph;
6. falls back to clone on invalid candidate output or invalid graph;
7. records operator and integrity telemetry.

This interface is already capable of learning ecology-conditioned switching
among conservative, parametric, structural, and mixed heredity. The stronger
benchmark and the direct program-lineage assay supply the stronger RSI test.

### 13.2 Gate for expanding the interface

The six-value MutationPlan is **not** a prerequisite. Implement it only when:

- the clonal-injury benchmark has passed;
- the four-score path has completed a small Shinka smoke search;
- candidates are valid and use different operators across ecological contexts;
- development evidence or operator telemetry indicates that one trusted
  mutation application is a material representational limit;
- implementation is estimated to fit within two focused development days;
- no TensorNEAT internal rewrite is required;
- compute-matched two-pass conventional baselines are included.

If any condition fails, retain the four-score interface and continue the core
experiment.

### 13.3 Optional six-value MutationPlan

If the gate passes, the candidate returns exactly:

```python
jnp.asarray(
    [
        clone_score,
        parametric_score,
        structural_score,
        mixed_score,
        extra_parametric_score,
        extra_structural_score,
    ],
    dtype=jnp.float32,
)
```

Trusted interpretation remains deterministic:

```text
argmax(first four values)
    → primary clone / parametric / structural / mixed

argmax([0, extra_parametric_score, extra_structural_score])
    → no extra / extra parametric / extra structural
```

Do not introduce softmax sampling. The existing argmax semantics are simpler,
replayable, and avoid coupling operator-selection randomness to mutation
randomness.

Trusted execution:

1. validate and clip all six values;
2. select the primary operator;
3. apply the primary trusted mutation;
4. transform and validate the intermediate CPPN;
5. select at most one optional extra mutation;
6. apply it with an independent key and, for structural mutation, an independent
   dynamic-node identity;
7. transform and validate the final CPPN;
8. fall back to the last valid intermediate genome if the extra stage fails;
9. fall back to the parent if the primary stage fails;
10. report both stages separately.

### 13.4 Identity-safe mutation context for two passes

If two passes are supported, extend `MutationContext` with independent mutation
keys and dynamic-node identities. A safe design reserves two node identities per
child:

```text
primary_new_node_key = DYNAMIC_NODE_KEY_OFFSET + 2 * child_id
extra_new_node_key   = DYNAMIC_NODE_KEY_OFFSET + 2 * child_id + 1
```

Update and test the exact-float32 identity bound. Never reuse the same structural
node identity for both passes. If globally unique two-pass identities cannot be
implemented without invasive TensorNEAT changes, drop the extra structural pass
or the entire MutationPlan.

### 13.5 Mutation-action budget parity

An extra mutation pass is additional search capability even when simulator cost
dominates runtime. Therefore, if the six-value plan is used:

- every policy receives the same maximum of two trusted mutation actions per
  birth;
- record actual mutation-kernel calls;

## 16. Score and evaluation metrics

### 16.1 Preserve the absolute primary score

Do not normalize by a candidate's own pre-injury productivity. A candidate could
otherwise make recovery appear large by performing poorly before the shock.

Retain the externally normalized score:

```text
P_chunk = cumulative fulfilled mouth uptake / (chunk_steps * dt)
P_ref   = sum(resource_regeneration_map after event)
q_chunk = clip(P_chunk / max(P_ref, epsilon), 0, 1)

episode_score   = mean(q_chunk over frozen post-event chunks)
candidate_score = equal-weight mean of founder-level means
```

Extinction naturally produces zero future productivity.

Do not add population, survival, diversity, recovery-time, mutation-size, or
code-complexity bonuses to the candidate score. These remain diagnostics.

### 16.2 Primary confirmatory contrast

Use exactly one primary confirmatory contrast:

> **Final punctuated-trained Shinka program minus the initial Shinka program on
> sealed injured-world normalized post-event productivity AUC.**

This is the direct bounded program-lineage improvement test.

### 16.3 Key secondary contrasts

1. final punctuated-trained program minus the strongest predeclared
   compute-matched conventional heredity baseline;
2. final punctuated-trained program minus final stable-trained program;
3. final punctuated-trained program minus the handwritten stress-responsive
   scheduler;
4. conventional mutation minus clone, confirming benchmark causality on sealed
   founders;
5. program parent/child effects along the actual champion ancestry path.

### 16.4 Secondary episode metrics

- paired injured-minus-sham productivity effect;
- immediate resistance after injury;
- final injured-world productivity;
- survival and extinction;
- censored time to regain a frozen fraction of sham productivity;
- births and post-event generation depth;
- primary/extra operator frequency;
- mutation-action count;
- parent–child genome distance;
- node and connection changes;
- invalid/fallback rate.

Lineage and action diversity remain explanatory diagnostics, not large reward
terms.

## 19. Direct program-lineage RSI assay

### 19.1 What counts as program ancestry

Use Shinka's actual `parent_id` chain from the frozen champion. Do not treat
archive inspirations, island copies, nearest code, or chronological generations
as direct ancestors.

The trusted lineage extractor must:

1. open the completed Shinka SQLite database read-only;
2. verify its hash against the freeze record;
3. obtain the declared champion program ID;
4. walk `ProgramDatabase.get_ancestry()` or equivalent parent links to the root;
5. collapse source-identical copies by source hash while preserving parent
   provenance;
6. export candidate ID, parent ID, generation, source hash, training score,
   patch description, and code diff;
7. hash the lineage export before sealed access.

### 19.2 Which programs to evaluate

The confirmatory comparison always includes:

- the initial root program;
- the final selected descendant.

If the distinct ancestry path is short, evaluate every distinct program. If it
is longer, select before sealed access:

- root;
- the closest distinct ancestor to one-third of ancestry depth;
- the closest distinct ancestor to two-thirds of ancestry depth;
- the champion's direct parent;
- the final champion.

Deduplicate coincident choices by source hash. Intermediate results are
descriptive; improvement need not be monotonic because archive search can retain
stepping stones.

### 19.3 Fresh-population evaluation

For each selected program:

1. discard every organism and state produced during outer search;
2. start fresh clonal ecosystems from sealed founder artifacts;
3. use identical paired ecological seeds and injury vectors;
4. use the same simulator and mutation-action budgets;
5. fork exact injured/sham twins at the event boundary;
6. evaluate the complete post-event adaptation curve;
7. retain all source, database, manifest, founder, simulator, and result hashes.

The central test is:

```text
sealed injured-world AUC(final program)
  - sealed injured-world AUC(initial program)
```

A positive paired interval supports bounded improvement of the heredity program.
It does not show that Shinka rewrote or improved itself.

### 19.4 Lineage plots

Produce:

- program ancestry graph with exact parent edges;
- source-code diffs along the selected chain;
- sealed score versus ancestry depth;
- paired recovery curves for root and final program;
- operator-use and mutation-magnitude traces around injury;
- cumulative best score versus proposal generations, fully valid candidate
  evaluations, and simulator calls.

Do not imply monotonic recursive improvement if intermediate programs regress.

## 20. Statistical analysis

### 20.1 Experimental units

- founder is the generalization cluster;
- founder×world-seed pair is the paired ecological observation;
- scenario/injury pattern is a stratum;
- outer Shinka run is the unit for claims about search-regime superiority;
- ancestor individual is the cluster for biological common gardens;
- three GPU reruns are nested numerical measurements, not ecological replicates;
- timesteps, organisms within one world, and descendants of one ancestor are not
  independent samples.

### 20.2 Numerical repeat protocol

GPU scatter/reduction order is not bitwise stable on the current backend.
Therefore:

- execute every complete policy/manifest evaluation exactly three times;
- require integrity in all three;
- select one coherent median-scoring complete execution with stable tie-breaking;
- retain that execution's entire episode vector;
- never median individual episodes or metrics across runs;
- never count the three executions as additional ecological observations.

Candidate function replay and CPU unit fixtures remain exactly deterministic
where applicable.

### 20.3 Hierarchical paired bootstrap

For each policy contrast:

1. compute direct paired effects for identical
   `founder_id × world_seed × injury_pattern` observations;
2. resample founders with replacement;
3. resample paired world seeds within each selected founder;
4. compute a mean within founder;
5. give founders equal weight in the aggregate;
6. repeat 10,000 times using one preregistered bootstrap seed;
7. report the paired mean, median, fraction favoring the candidate, per-founder
   effects, and percentile 95% interval.

Do not substitute many seeds from two founders for founder-level transfer.
Report interval width honestly.

### 20.4 Interpretation

- primary interval entirely above zero: evidence that the final program improved
  over its program ancestor for this sealed founder panel;
- interval overlapping zero: no reliable evidence of improvement;
- final beats initial but not the strongest conventional policy: bounded program
  lineage improved, but it did not establish a superior heredity algorithm;
- punctuated beats stable in one outer-search pair: evidence for this matched
  case, not a general law of punctuated meta-evolution;
- do not use p-values or multiplicity machinery unless an external review
  requires them; transparent paired effects are preferred.

## 21. Conditional biological ancestor–descendant assay

Run this only if the final program shows a positive main adaptive result.

### 21.1 Capture

For selected sealed injury worlds:

- save every organism alive at the event boundary: individual ID, parent ID,
  founder ID, raw CPPN node genes, and raw connection genes;
- enable finalist birth tracing after injury;
- at a frozen post-injury generation/time, select descendants;
- traverse parent edges to each descendant's exact event-time ancestor.

### 21.2 Common gardens

Evaluate each ancestor and descendant separately on the same standardized body,
position, energy, age, resource field, and assay keys. Disable reproduction and
mutation. Recompute graph caches in trusted code.

Use two gardens:

1. healthy actuator gains of all ones;
2. the sealed injured actuator-gain vector.

Compute:

```text
injured_effect = descendant_injured - ancestor_injured
healthy_effect = descendant_healthy - ancestor_healthy
interaction    = injured_effect - healthy_effect
```

Average descendants within exact ancestor, then ancestors within world, then
worlds within founder. A positive injured effect shows heritable improvement in
the changed regime. A positive interaction is stronger evidence that the change
is specifically adaptive to the injury rather than generally higher-performing.

## 22. Conditional mechanism ablation

If the final program has a positive paired advantage, write a mechanism
hypothesis before ablation. Use the smallest one-factor change that tests the
observed code mechanism, for example:

- replace one ecological statistic with a neutral value;
- freeze dynamic operator scores to their development-set mean action;
- remove one stress-conditioned branch or interaction term;
- disable the optional extra mutation while retaining the primary schedule;
- force the final program's most common fixed operator.

Verify from realized mutation telemetry that the ablation changed only the
intended mechanism. If designed after sealed results, run it on a fresh post-final
panel and label it exploratory. Never reopen or repurpose the sealed panel.

## 28. Functional requirements

- FR-1: `EcosystemState` stores one fixed-shape local-hinge actuator-gain vector.
- FR-2: every bending command path is multiplied by the active gain vector before
  modifying bending rest angles.
- FR-3: actuation energy is charged from intended rather than effective command.
- FR-4: actuator injury persists across all later births.
- FR-5: strict manifest validation includes exact injury vectors and hashes.
- FR-6: environment reset accepts one immutable founder genome independent of
  world seed.
- FR-7: clonal reset broadcasts identical genomes while preserving the existing
  per-initial-individual pedigree-lineage identity.
- FR-8: original diverse-founder initialization remains available.
- FR-9: founder artifacts use non-pickle numeric storage and SHA-256 verification.
- FR-10: training, development, and sealed founder partitions are disjoint.
- FR-11: development and sealed evaluation use exact pre-event injury/sham forks.
- FR-12: Shinka candidate code never executes mutation directly.
- FR-13: the four-score scheduler remains supported even if an optional six-score
  ABI is added.
- FR-14: optional multi-pass mutation uses independent RNG and node-identity keys.
- FR-15: optional multi-pass policies and baselines share a fixed maximum mutation
  action budget.
- FR-16: candidate, graph, event, founder, and run integrity failures fail closed.
- FR-17: the actual Shinka parent chain is exported and hashed before sealed.
- FR-18: final evaluation starts new ecosystems and discards outer-search
  organism states.
- FR-19: results aggregate within founder before equal-weight founder aggregation.
- FR-20: every frozen artifact and permission transition is recorded.

## 29. Non-functional requirements

- NFR-1: all ecology state shapes remain static under JIT.
- NFR-2: actuator patterns and founders do not trigger recompilation when only
  array values change.
- NFR-3: focused GPU jobs remain inside the 24 GiB guarded-process limit.
- NFR-4: no new third-party runtime or statistics dependency is introduced.
- NFR-5: search telemetry remains compact enough not to materialize per-birth
  histories for every candidate.
- NFR-6: existing pilot manifests and hashes remain byte-identical.
- NFR-7: candidate output is deterministic for identical bounded input.
- NFR-8: GPU reproducibility is defined by identical specifications, complete
  triplicate measurements, and bounded dispersion—not bitwise trajectory equality.
- NFR-9: every implementation phase leaves the existing ecosystem runnable.
- NFR-10: no hidden-test information is reachable from candidate or proposer
  processes.

## 30. Stop conditions

Stop or fall back rather than broadening scope when:

- no predefined injury gain creates a survivable mutation-sensitive benchmark;
- the benchmark passes training but fails the preregistered development
  confirmation;
- persistent injury cannot be applied to newborns without changing core physics;
- founder injection causes dynamic shapes or repeated compilation;
- the optional MutationPlan requires TensorNEAT internal rewriting;
- independent structural identities for two passes cannot be guaranteed;
- fallback or invalid-child rates exceed the frozen acceptance threshold;
- candidate changes materially increase recompilation or runtime;
- mutation-action budgets cannot be matched across policies;
- holdout access cannot be kept sealed;
- final source/config/manifest hashes cannot be frozen and verified;
- GPU memory requirements exceed the existing 24 GiB guard.

The fallback is the existing four-score scheduler on the stronger clonal-injury
benchmark. The fallback is not a temporary fixed controller, a new simulator, or
an unrestricted edit surface.

### 31.2 Minimum scientific success

- the benchmark passes Tier A;
- the final punctuated program shows a positive paired sealed advantage over its
  initial program ancestor.

This supports bounded program-lineage improvement for the studied founder panel.

### 31.3 Strong result

- the final punctuated program also beats the strongest compute-matched
  conventional and handwritten policies;
- it beats the stable-trained champion in the matched selected-search case;
- its code uses an understandable ecology-conditioned mechanism;
- one ablation weakens the advantage;
- post-injury biological descendants beat their exact ancestors, ideally with a
  positive injury-specific interaction.

### 31.4 Valid negative result

If Tier A passes but the final program does not beat its initial ancestor, report:

> The redesigned benchmark causally required newly generated heredity, but this
> bounded ShinkaEvolve search did not reliably improve the heredity program over
> its program ancestor under the tested candidate budget.

If Tier A itself fails, report:

> Persistent actuator degradation and clonal initialization did not yield a
> survivable benchmark in which the trusted conventional mutation operators
> reliably outperformed cloning within the predefined calibration grid.

Do not change metrics, founders, or sealed disturbances to manufacture success.
